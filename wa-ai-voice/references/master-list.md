# The Master List — every slop rule, consolidated

This is the superset. `tells.md` remains the fast catalog; this file is what a full audit loads. The cluster rule still governs Part A: one tell is noise, three within ~100 words is a rewrite signal. A bound voice's observed rates override every cap in here. Protected shapes stay protected.

---

## PART A — TELLS (what to hunt in the text)

### A1. Vocabulary

- General LLM lexicon: delve, intricate/intricacies, pivotal, testament, underscore, boast (features), vibrant, crucial, comprehensive, robust, seamless(ly), leverage (verb), foster, harness, navigate/landscape/journey/realm/tapestry (metaphorical), unlock, elevate, game-changer, cutting-edge, ever-evolving, multifaceted, holistic, myriad, plethora, dive into / deep dive, unpack, at its core, the beauty of, "in today's fast-paced world".
- Connective tissue: sentence-initial Additionally/Moreover/Furthermore/Indeed; "It's worth noting"; "It's important to note/remember"; reflexive "That said,".
- Chatbot artifacts (instant fail): "I hope this helps", "Great question", "Certainly!", "Let's explore", "In this article we will", leftover "As an AI".
- Startup/insider fake-smart: rails, distribution (as flex), infrastructure (as vibe), ecosystem, "the tell", "the whole game", market structure, synergy, unearned alpha, "X is the new Y", "the use case nobody's talking about", slide-title phrasing.
- LinkedIn/announcement cluster: "excited to announce", "passionate about", "results-driven", "in today's landscape", "the intersection of", "at the end of the day".
- Hype-intensifier adjectives: "flawless", "a different league", "terrifyingly good", "the scary part?", "this changes everything", "let that sink in".
- Dated in-group filler: wagmi, ngmi, "let me cook", "this is the way", gm/gn posts, "great thread ser", "your money works while you sleep", "actual alpha".
- Engagement-bait hooks: "BREAKING", "you won't believe", all-caps hooks, "if you're reading this, you're early".
- Stock platform-native reaction filler (human-sourced but anonymous): "been saying this for years", "obsessed with this", "doing a LOT of work here", "nobody warns you about this part", "someone confirm".
- Borrowed circulating templates: "nice try officer", "X with extra steps", "they know what they did" — a phrase any account could paste fails even when the move is right.
- Generic-agreement filler (instant cut): "so true", "this is huge", "couldn't agree more".
- Pseudo-analytic scaffolds: "is the right X", "the next question is".
- Motivational-hustle / guru genre as a whole: grind, rise-and-grind, "if you're not doing X you're ngmi".

### A2. Structure

- Negative parallelism: "It's not just X, it's Y" / "not only... but also".
- False contrast: two things opposed that aren't in tension, or a strawman on one side ("rather than", "instead of", "the question isn't A but B"). The form is an argument; the substance isn't.
- Buried lead: the point arrives in paragraph four or at the end of the section. In any piece meant to move a decision, the bottom line goes first.
- Evidence stacking: evidence → evidence → evidence → conclusion. Invert it — claim, then support.
- Detail in the body that the argument doesn't need: methodology, full tables, rosters. Appendix.
- Rule of three everywhere; humans vary count.
- False range: "from X to Y" over non-scalar things.
- Setup + payoff where the payoff restates the setup.
- Synonym cycling: same referent renamed each mention.
- Copula avoidance: "serves as", "stands as", "acts as", "represents" for "is".
- Uniform sentence length; low burstiness. Sanding rhythm below the AI baseline is itself a machine signal (benchmark-proven).
- Fake question pivots: "The result?" "The best part?" "Why does this matter?".
- Aphorism closer / writerly kicker: crafted symmetrical constructions, meta-commentary on your own sentence ("the least surprising word in that sentence"). Closers are blunt withheld words or raw exasperation, never a crafted aphorism.
- Hedging seesaw: bold claim immediately softened.
- Em-dash chains (cluster member only).
- Colon headline: "X: Why Y Matters".
- Format announcing: "a timeline:", "update on the saga:", "sharing...", "and here's why", framing headers, setup labels. Open mid-scene on the first receipt.
- Labeled joke scaffolds: "good news:/bad news:", comedian formats. Twists live inside plain prose beats.
- Framing preambles: "remember when i said...". Open on the payoff or the label.
- Post-kicker coda: any beat added after the punch. The last line is the flattest and SHORTEST beat. If the closer is the longest line, cut it under ~8 words or move the punch up.
- Trailing softeners/particles: "eh", "irl", "tbh", "ngl", "no?", "too", "as well" — unless the particle IS the joke. End on the last load-bearing word.
- Third-person self-description drift: "a marketer with...", "a content creator that..." — first person always, even inside meme templates.
- Moral/lesson extraction: appending a takeaway, resolving what the author leaves unresolved. Flat-unsure endings are a legitimate shape.
- "thoughts?" / "what do you think?" closers; bare CTA; naked link with no reason to care; trailing "save this" / bookmark bait.
- Square-bracket placeholders in a finished draft.
- Reskin of the author's own existing piece with words swapped: kill it, take a different angle.

### A3. Formatting

- Mechanical boldface; emoji bullets; Title Case Headers; bullets where an argument needed prose; "In conclusion" sections; tables for non-enumerable things; horizontal rules every four lines; numbered headers in casual contexts.
- ALL CAPS for promotional emphasis reads as selling.
- Foreign bullet glyph: using • when the author uses - or >. Bind the author's list marker.
- Platform mechanics count as slop: a leading bare @-mention scopes a post as a reply and kills reach. Check format mechanics, not just style.
- Media captions: never narrate what the media already shows; use the author's caption grammar (present-tense register, "me opening this email..."), not past-tense recap.

### A4. Stance

- False balance on questions that have answers.
- Sycophancy: agreeing with the premise before answering.
- Fake enthusiasm: exclamation marks on routine facts.
- No stake: no opinion that could be wrong, no taste, no enemy.
- Restating as engaging: summarizing the source back in smoother words.
- Comprehensiveness reflex: covering all aspects when the reader needed one answer.
- Moralizing: tacked-on lessons the author would never draw.
- Detached-observer framing: writing as anthropologist about a world the author writes from inside. When a bit has legs, escalate INTO it; match the author's emotional temperature, never damp it.
- Explaining the joke, or the implied point, or context the reader is trusted to have. Never hedge shared knowledge ("something called X" for a thing the audience knows).
- Identity-label framing ("as a woman in tech"): show the stance, don't announce the category.
- Branded copy as feature list: a branded piece must be a first-person observation about USING the thing, never the brief restated. "thank me later" energy = ad mode.
- Fortune-cookie lines: standalone-clever quotables that don't need the specific context are slop dressed as wit, EVEN when the writing is sharp. Clever is not a defense against interchangeable.
- Dead memes and topic-label replies.
- Forcing the product/brand where it doesn't belong.

### A5. Delivery mechanics (where clones actually fail — in one backtest, 9/28 missed the punchline, 7/28 the opener)

- Never euphemize the blunt word: "fired" not "lost my job". Profanity and harsh nouns are the joke's engine; profane closes stay in.
- Never spell-correct a name, fix grammar agreement, or smooth vocabulary to normie synonyms. Quoted dialogue stays verbatim. A draft that would pass an English test is a red flag.
- Preserve clause-joining rhythm: don't period-chop run-ons into staccato, don't merge the author's line breaks.
- Real-time reaction register stays ONE breathless run-on; never structure spontaneity into multi-beat formats.
- The one place looseness is banned: the first word / hook line. A botched opener kills the piece regardless of content.
- Length zones come from the corpus, including dead middle bands: never write "medium". Length above the default must be earned (a receipt or a rage). Draft natural, cut 20-30% — and the cut comes out of the MIDDLE, never the last line.
- Delete any line the piece survives without; the author stops one beat earlier than feels finished.
- One language per unit; no codeswitching inside a single piece unless the corpus shows it.
- Anti-polish must be enforced at draft time, not stated as a prohibition — clones ignore the written rule; leave some drafts per batch with unpolished syntax.

## PART B — VOICE BINDING (how a voice card gets built)

- Count before you describe; adjectives forbidden in the counting phase. A description cannot be wrong; a rate can.
- Every card claim carries a receipt: a count or a quoted sample with its date. No receipt, cut the claim.
- Hard rule vs tendency: broken >5% of the time = tendency, label it so.
- LOW-N discipline: any pattern with n<30 gets flagged and produces no rule.
- Interchangeability test applies to the card itself: an observation the author could answer "true of anyone" is a compliment, cut it.
- Card the absences: what they never do, how the flex arrives, who the joke targets, where they get serious and what signals it, their stated-and-broken contradiction, what they do with nothing to say.
- Collect flops as anti-anchors, plus the modal/median piece — not a highlight reel. Separate delivery-artifact flops (deleted/reposted) from genuine content flops first. Exclude the pre-voice era.
- One card per organ: long pieces and quick replies are different voices with contradictory load-bearing rules. A merged file turns one format's rule into the other's bug.
- Match the DISTRIBUTION, not the mode: corpus 50/50 on casing means the batch is ~50/50, mapped to the register that licenses each variant. A batch that is 100% the majority variant fails.
- Don't select samples by topic similarity to the task (measurably reduces style match); 2→10 exemplars adds little; weight openers heaviest (openings carry 15-50pp of the style signal).
- Known LLM drop-list — check these survived: ellipses, non-standard capitalization, niche slang.
- ESL/direct-translation idiom is voice, not error. Dialect level, never textbook-neutral: the author's regional variant, never the textbook default; laughter/reaction tokens are language-coded independently of the text's language.
- Corpora are non-stationary: report last-90-day rates alongside lifetime wherever they differ; retire dead bits — a clone doing a dead bit is a dated clone.
- The card includes "what I could not determine": unobservable modalities, missing context, media-blindness.
- The author's stated intent outranks their measured corpus. A dropped rule is deleted, not softened. ("correct my grammar mistakes please" beats the corpus.)
- Correction weighting: one explicit author edit is admissible alone; a vague category complaint needs three.
- The author's own version, written BEFORE seeing yours, is the only uncontaminated ground truth. Never show your draft first.
- Re-read the whole card at every invocation, not the remembered version: voice drifts across a chat, always toward the model default.
- Lane fit is separate from voice fit: topic rhyming is not lane fit; off-lane pieces read as anyone's and dilute the byline.

## PART C — GATES AND REFUSALS

- Zero specifics → the correct output is the sentence "anything I write here will be generic", not a draft. Never invent particulars. A fact about the author is admissible only if the author published it; keep a particular bank with source and date.
- Topic-fit refusal: if the brief's genre is one the author mocks rather than performs, decline and name the nearest lane that works. Producing a competent off-lane draft is the failure.
- Reactive text with missing source: if the parent/thing responded to is absent, truncated, or vague — STOP and ask. Never reconstruct it.
- A process-correct refusal scores as a PASS, not a throughput loss. Confirm with the principal that refusal behavior is wanted (it trades throughput for zero invention).
- Over-refusal is also a failure: refusing a safe request because it contains trigger-shaped vocabulary is broken. Test both directions.
- Persona binding degrades refusal behavior 50-70%: a voice-bound draft is MORE likely to comply with something the author would decline. The refusal check must be explicit, never assumed.
- Register traps to stress-test: promotional pressure collapses clones into marketer voice (the flex must arrive the way the author's flexes arrive); serious register is not jokes-but-quieter; second languages must be the regional register.
- Every checkable claim used (metrics, dates, names, appearances) gets flagged for author verification before ship.
- Some voice features need explicit human sign-off before an agent ships them under a real name: profanity retention, slur-adjacent register present in the corpus, deliberately introduced typos.

## PART D — AUDIT INSTRUMENT (how to judge without lying to yourself)

- Never ask a model — including yourself — "does this sound like me/them?". The answer is shaped by what the asker wants and is not evidence.
- Self-judgment is biased in the same direction as the defect: judges reward low-perplexity, over-typical text, so the too-smooth draft is exactly what self-scoring under-penalizes. This survives every wall. Fresh context is necessary but not sufficient.
- Absolute scores don't replicate: the same text scored 6.18 and 7.64 by fresh judges; a ~1.5-point swing flipped a shipping verdict. Prefer forced choice against real samples, counted failures, and paired discrimination rates over any absolute number. Never score out of 100; pattern-match instead.
- Score the MOVE, not word overlap: lexical similarity earns nothing, structural fidelity everything.
- Competent-but-generic has a hard ceiling: clean execution anyone could produce caps low regardless of polish.
- Score delivery mechanics as separate named dimensions (opener family, length zone, punchline placement + dryness, lane, language) — failures concentrate there, not in genericness.
- Require every verdict to state what decided it: bare A/B answers ship false findings (a judge once decided on HTML escapes and shortened links, zero prose read). Normalize formatting artifacts on both sides first.
- Discount leakage: a hit caused by the brief containing the answer is part leakage, not skill. Grade only against context the drafter had.
- Audit the SET, not just each piece: drafts can each pass while clustering far tighter than the author's real spread. Compare intra-set variance to the author's own.
- Style match and detectability are separate axes: 99.9% style agreement can remain separable on perplexity alone.
- Candidate selection is its own failure mode: the correct draft is often generated then killed. Log why the survivor won; check killed alternates against documented move families.
- Validate any new binding by backtest: hold out real pieces, draft blind from situation cards, judge pairwise, per-format (never pool), against an anchored calibration set. Trap-test before trusting: off-lane topic, zero-fact brief, missing-parent reply — passing means refusing or asking.

## PART E — EDIT SCOPE AND CARVE-OUTS

- "Fix the tone" means phrasing only, never substance. Protect the author's raw phrasing; it is often better than the polished rewrite.
- Deliver 2-3 variants and name your pick with one line of why. Don't hand over one polished thing.
- Legitimate-generic carve-out: some slots are maintenance by design (acknowledgements, thank-you upkeep). Don't force a distinctive line into them, don't chase a banger on every unit. INTERCHANGEABLE does not apply to these slots.
- The screenshot-line test for pieces that ARE meant to land: every banger has one line someone wants to capture. If there isn't one, find or build it.
- Protected shapes (from tells.md) still stand: the author's signature moves pattern-match tells and are never flagged. The distinction: a tell restates or pads; a signature lands somewhere else.
- Max two fix passes. Sanding past that is the new tell. Deletion-to-neutral is half a fix; every replacement moves toward the author's words.
- Report counted failures and a routing verdict, never a summed score. PART D above applies to this skill's own instrument: an absolute number implies a precision the count does not have.
