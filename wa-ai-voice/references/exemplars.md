# Exemplars — Watershed work product that hits the bar

Three published deliverables. Unlike `anti-examples.md`, these are external documents, so client and state names stay: the specificity is the point being demonstrated.

Read this file before auditing. The anti-examples set a floor; these set the target, and they are the source of every observed rate in `watershed-ece-voice.md` §5.

**Caution on n.** PE-1 and PE-2 share a five-state comparison table nearly verbatim. That is a reused house asset, which strengthens "this is a house move" and weakens "these are three independent samples." Likely one or two authors across all three. Rates below are real observations; register questions they can't settle are listed as still-open in the house voice §6.

## PE-1 — Texas early childhood landscape analysis. Deck, 31 slides, 2024

Landscape analysis and recommendations for a philanthropic client on the state's early childhood system.

**Slide titles are claims, and this is the cleanest demonstration of the deck rubric in the corpus.** Not "Economic Impact" but *"This is an economic problem. Texas loses more than $9 billion annually from its economy due to breakdowns in child care."* Then *"This is a school readiness problem. Only about half of third graders…"* Then *"This is a good government problem. Texas ranked 44th…"* Three consecutive slides, each naming the problem type and immediately paying it off with the number. Read the titles alone and you have the argument.

**One hero number per slide.** `-$9.39B` · `Only 50%` · `44th`. Displayed large, sourced in a footnote with publisher and year, and supported by three or four sub-points beneath. Never two hero numbers competing on one slide.

**The insight chain is the deck's spine.** Challenge → System Fragmentation (the cause) → Ideal State (where it leads) → Recommendations (what to do). That is the INSIGHT gate rendered as a table of contents. Slide 10 makes the hinge explicit in one sentence: *"This is a result of system fragmentation."*

**Parallel structure with full cells.** Slide 22 runs five consequences on identical labels — Ineffective Data, Inconsistent Experiences, Overburdened Families, Unsupported Employers, Broken Child Care Market — each with a real sentence of content beneath it. Compare AE-1 slide 6: eight parallel labels, nothing beneath any of them. **Same shape, opposite outcome. Parallelism is not the failure; empty cells are.**

**Local proof beats assertion.** Two "Spotlight on" slides name Fort Worth and the Texas Panhandle with organizations (Child Care Associates of Tarrant County, Amarillo Area Foundation), dates (2019, 2021), and counts (26 counties, four with zero providers, 14 child care deserts). Then the honest turn: *"While promising, these local efforts are limited by the misalignment and inefficiency in the funding and regulations in the state system."* The example is used to make an argument, not to decorate.

**Recommendations are verb-first and bounded.** *"Unify all early care and education programs… into one streamlined, accountable system." "Pilot a model of regional leadership…" "Require that…" "Collect, report, and use data…"* Prefaced with *"Texas leaders should at a minimum"* — a floor, stated as a floor.

**Logged honestly:** slide 14 carries `"Insert quote from parent here" - Parent`. A placeholder in a positive exemplar. It's a working deck, not a monument, and the bar is not immaculateness.

## PE-2 — The Plan for TN Tomorrow. Report, ~50 pages, 2024

A statewide governance plan produced for an advocacy organization. The most complete demonstration of the house structure.

**The "?" stat box — the best single artifact in this corpus.** Four hero numbers in a row: `-$2.6B`, `60%`, `43rd`, and `?`. The fourth reads: *"Status of kindergarten readiness for Tennessee children. Though a critical metric, Tennessee has no consistent way of evaluating or tracking kindergarten readiness."*

The unknown is given the same visual weight as the $2.6 billion. It is not hedged, footnoted, or moved to an appendix — it is promoted to a headline, and it becomes an argument for the recommendation. This is the exact inverse of AE-3, which buried its caveats in a validation appendix while presenting a complete architecture in the body. **Where does the document admit it doesn't know something? Here, in 48-point type.**

**Abstract-then-anchor — the move analysts should copy first.** Every benefit claim is generic on its own and immediately cashed out:

> **Streamlined Operations and Efficiency:** Consolidating programs under a single agency reduces fragmentation and eliminates the duplication of administrative processes…
> **Current State:** Tennessee currently has two teams conducting child care monitoring—one at TDOE and one at TDHS.

Five benefits, five `Current State:` receipts, every one naming a Tennessee agency or a Tennessee number. The abstraction is not the failure — an unanchored abstraction is. **This is why INTERCHANGEABLE must read forward; scored in isolation, every one of those benefit sentences fails.**

**Options presented, one chosen, out loud.** Three unification approaches in a table with benefits *and* challenges for each — including the challenges of the recommended one (*"Risk of Early Childhood Taking a Back Burner"*). Then: *"After careful evaluation, this report recommends unifying early care and education programs under TDOE."* Compare AE-2, which listed ten open questions and ranked none.

**Committed verbs.** *"Tennessee must unify…"* · *"it is essential Tennessee lawmakers move to unify programs into one agency"* · *"relentlessly driving toward a vision."* The word "relentlessly" appears repeatedly and is doing stance work.

**Recommendation sections open on an italicized thesis** before any supporting material, and close with a short bolded conclusion restating the ask. BLUF at section level, not just document level.

**Evidence types, in descending frequency:** hyperlinked source on the number itself · named practitioner quotes with title and organization (including from comparison states) · provider and parent quotes from focus groups, verbatim and unpolished · comparison tables · logic models (Challenge → Causes → Strategy → Immediate/Intermediate/Long-term Outcome).

## PE-3 — St. Louis ECE implementation plan. Report, ~40 pages, 2025

Regional implementation plan for a philanthropic and civic coalition.

**Costed commitment.** *"Achieving this vision at scale will take an estimated $580 million in new local funding each year, a bold and ambitious target."* The number is stated, then named as ambitious rather than softened. Year-by-year build-out follows with headcounts (9–10 staff in year 1 rising to 19–21 by year 5) and annual costs ($1.5–2M rising to $3.8–4M). Every phase has a dollar figure and a staffing plan.

**Defined terms carry their own numbers.** *"We define 'economically disadvantaged' as children from households at or below 185% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). In 2025, this equates to roughly $60,000 for a family of four."* The definition is given, then translated into a number a reader can picture.

**The gap is quantified two ways.** *"Of the almost 30,000 children eligible… fewer than 10,000 have access to publicly funded early childhood seats. This means that nearly 20,000 low-income children cannot access essential early learning opportunities."* Ratio, then absolute count. The reader gets both.

**A current event used as evidence, not color.** *"Earlier this year, tornadoes devastated our region, shutting down child care programs and leaving families without options, just as we experienced during the pandemic. These crises exposed how fragile the early childhood system is."* Specific, recent, and load-bearing for the resilience argument.

**Guiding principles are written as instructions, not values.** *"Coordinate, Don't Complicate."* Compare AE-1's *"A stronger start for every child."*

## The positive moves — adopted

**1. Abstraction is allowed when it is immediately cashed out.** Pair every general claim with a named local specific — an agency, a count, a date, a named program. The house pattern is claim → `Current State:` receipt. Analysts should not be told to avoid abstraction; they should be told to anchor it in the next sentence.

**2. Promote the unknown, don't bury it.** A gap the analysis could not close gets stated at the weight of the findings around it, and turned into an argument. The `?` in a row of hero numbers is the canonical form.

**3. One hero number per unit, sourced.** Publisher and year in a footnote; the link on the number itself in prose documents.

**4. Present options, then pick — and name the cost of the pick.** Benefits and challenges for every option including the recommended one. The recommendation appears in its own sentence, in bold or italics, unhedged.

**5. Titles and section heads make claims.** *"This is an economic problem. Texas loses more than $9 billion…"* not *"Economic Impact."*

**6. Parallel structure is fine — empty cells are not.** If a structure has five slots, five slots have content. If only three have content, the structure has three slots.

**7. Commit with a number and a date.** Recommendations carry costs, headcounts, phases, and years. "$580 million annually, a bold and ambitious target" is the register: state the number, name it ambitious, don't soften it.

**8. Quote real people, unpolished.** Named practitioners with title and organization; providers and parents verbatim, including run-ons and rough grammar. Quoted material is never cleaned up.

**9. Reuse the house library.** The five-state comparison table appears in PE-1 and PE-2 with the same columns. Matching the voice includes matching the assets — check whether a table, logic model, or framing already exists before inventing one.
